# Partner Creative Review Rubric

Use this rubric as an issue finder, not a substitute for the current Point brand, content, product, or compliance sources. Apply each relevant section and report only findings supported by the artifact or a named evidence gap.

## 1. Point identity and brand relationship

Check whether:

- every Point logo is an approved source asset rather than typed, redrawn, reconstructed, or AI-generated
- the logo's artwork, proportions, colors, clear space, contrast, and minimum size follow the current `point-brand-guidelines` skill
- a logo remains legible over photography or texture; use an approved alternate variant or suitable background treatment when needed
- a partner mark has replaced the Point mark or created an unofficial Point sub-brand
- Point typefaces are used when the work is Point-branded or the intended cobranding requires a visible Point connection
- a partner-led visual system remains clear about Point's role and leads credibly to the stated destination
- the palette, typography, CTA, footer, social-proof bar, and repeated components are internally consistent across a creative set

Do not hardcode a color, logo version, typeface, or layout from a historical review. Compare against the current approved source. A partner palette or typeface is not inherently wrong in partner-branded creative.

Common obvious issues include an AI-distorted logo that changes between variants, a text approximation of the logo, an unreadable logo over a photo, conflicting Point logo colorways within one set, and a footer whose width, spacing, or alignment changes without purpose.

## 2. Copy and product explanation

Check whether:

- the HEI is explained accurately without implying that it is a loan, debt-free money, free money, or a guaranteed outcome
- comparative phrases such as “not a loan,” “unlike a loan,” or “without adding debt” have current approval rather than relying on a technically plausible inference
- strong benefits such as no monthly payments or no interest charges are paired with enough repayment or tradeoff context to remain credible and understandable
- prequalification and application credit-check language are distinguished; a general “no credit hit” claim is not used when only a particular stage avoids a hard inquiry
- eligibility, qualification, ownership, property, income, credit, term, amount, timing, availability, and state claims match current approved sources
- social proof, ratings, customer counts, awards, and product limits are current and sourced
- targeting language does not introduce a false requirement or an unintended product association, such as implying outright home ownership is required or making an HEI sound like a reverse mortgage
- the headline, body, bullets, and CTA use a coherent point of view and one primary message
- urgency, rejection, or financial-stress framing remains accurate, measured, and respectful
- unusual product phrases are clear, current, and approved rather than merely clever

Flag stale or unsupported numbers as **P2 — Verify or route** rather than replacing them from memory. Do not report an optional copy improvement in the default review unless clarity or trust is materially compromised.

## 3. Disclosures, licensing, and qualifications

Check for the presence, legibility, and relationship of potentially required elements, including:

- NMLS or other licensing identifiers
- Equal Housing Opportunity marks
- claim-specific qualifications and footnotes
- state-specific language or availability
- testimonial, rating, or social-proof attribution
- eligibility, prequalification, credit-check, amount, timing, and repayment qualifications

Assess observable omissions and legibility. Route the requirement itself and all final language to Compliance or Legal. Do not infer that an element is optional because a prior asset omitted it, or required everywhere because one prior review requested it.

## 4. Destination and customer journey

Check whether:

- every CTA, button, yes/no choice, QR code, and text link has a known destination
- the creative accurately describes what happens after the click
- the brand presentation before and after the click does not create avoidable confusion or credibility concerns
- a transition from a partner property to a Point property is clear enough for the context
- the landing page, prequalification flow, or application stage matches credit-check, qualification, and CTA claims
- required leaving-site notices, consent treatments, or other journey controls have been confirmed by the appropriate owner

Unknown behavior is **P2 — Verify or route**. A general best practice is not automatically a Point or legal requirement.

## 5. Production quality and consistency

Check whether:

- repeated elements align and scale consistently across variants
- benefits have been prioritized rather than packed into an unreadable list
- disclaimers and badges are legible without overpowering the primary message
- the CTA is proportionate, credible, and visually connected to the hierarchy
- spacing, alignment, cropping, contrast, and typography remain deliberate at the intended size
- obvious artifacts, altered logos, malformed text, or other generative-AI defects are present
- the set looks intentional enough to support trust in a financial product

Do not report subjective polish in the default review. Use **P1 — Required fix** only when the execution creates a clear legibility, identity, consistency, or credibility defect.

## 6. Context and review ownership

Before objecting to audience-specific messaging or partner-led styling, consider the target, traffic source, intent signal, format, performance context, and stated brand mode. Targeting itself is not a defect.

Route findings by owner:

- Brand/Design: logo, visual identity, cobranding, composition, consistency, and production craft
- Content: voice, positioning, message hierarchy, product explanation, and audience framing
- Compliance/Legal: claims, qualifications, disclosures, licensing, state rules, and approval
- Partnerships/Growth: partner intent, channel context, destinations, operational ownership, and feedback delivery

## Calibration examples

- The Point logo visibly changes shape across three AI-generated ads: **P0 — Blocker** when the logo is fabricated; otherwise **P1 — Required fix**. Replace every instance with the same approved source asset.
- The Point logo sits unreadably over a bright sky: **P1 — Required fix**. Use an approved contrasting variant or a suitable background treatment.
- Partner-branded creative uses the partner's own typography consistently: no finding unless it misrepresents Point, conflicts with the intended cobranding, or creates a credibility problem at handoff.
- Two variants use different footer widths, button colors, and social-proof alignment: **P1 — Required fix** when the differences appear accidental.
- Copy says “no credit hit” but sends directly to an application: **P2 — Verify or route**. Confirm the stage and replace the claim with current approved language.
- Copy leads heavily with no monthly payments while omitting how Point is repaid: **P1 — Required fix**. Add current approved repayment context.
- A state-specific asset may lack a licensing element: **P2 — Verify or route**. Identify the observable omission and ask Compliance to confirm the requirement and language.
- A benefits list feels long but remains accurate and legible: no default finding. Offer a shorter list only when the user asks for optional craft feedback.
